# Joint Account Rules

A joint account can be opened by two or more individuals, most commonly spouses, family members, or business partners, and each holder must independently complete KYC verification before the account is activated. The mode of operation, chosen at account opening, determines how transactions are authorized: "either or survivor" allows any one holder to operate the account independently, while "jointly" requires all named holders to sign off on transactions. In the event of one holder's death, the "either or survivor" and "former or survivor" modes allow the surviving holder(s) to continue operating the account, whereas a purely joint mode may require the account to be reconstituted before continued use. Any holder can request the addition or removal of a joint holder, but such a change requires the written consent of all existing account holders. Loans and credit cards issued against a joint account typically make all holders jointly and severally liable for the outstanding dues, regardless of who initiated the transaction.
