# NRI Account Eligibility

A Non-Resident Indian is eligible to open an NRE (Non-Resident External) or NRO (Non-Resident Ordinary) account, and the choice between them depends on the source of the funds: an NRE account is meant for foreign earnings and is fully repatriable along with tax-free interest in India, while an NRO account is used to manage income earned within India, such as rent or dividends, and carries applicable Indian tax deductions. Opening either account requires proof of NRI status, such as a valid passport with a visa or work permit, an overseas address proof, and a PAN card. Funds can be deposited into an NRE account only in foreign currency or through transfers from another NRE account, whereas an NRO account accepts both foreign remittances and India-sourced income. NRIs are also eligible to apply for home loans in India, subject to additional documentation such as a power of attorney if the applicant cannot be physically present for the process. Account holders must promptly update their residency status if it changes, since operating a resident savings account after becoming an NRI is not permitted under regulatory rules.
