# Prepayment Penalty Rules

Floating-rate loans taken by individual borrowers for non-business purposes, such as most home and auto loans, cannot carry a prepayment or foreclosure penalty under current regulatory guidelines, whether the prepayment is partial or full. Fixed-rate loans and loans taken for business purposes may still attract a prepayment penalty, typically between 2% and 5% of the outstanding principal, to compensate the lender for the interest income lost. Personal loans commonly restrict prepayment during an initial lock-in period, often the first six to twelve months, after which partial or full prepayment is permitted subject to the applicable fee. Partial prepayments are usually capped at a maximum percentage of the outstanding principal per year, beyond which the excess amount may still be accepted but charged as if it were a full foreclosure. Any applicable prepayment fee is disclosed upfront in the loan's key fact statement so the borrower can factor it into their repayment planning.
