# Credit Score Impact Factors

Payment history is the single largest factor influencing a credit score, so even one missed or late EMI or credit card payment can cause a noticeable drop that takes several months of on-time payments to recover from. Credit utilization, meaning the proportion of the available credit limit currently being used, is the next major factor, and consistently keeping utilization below 30% of the total limit helps maintain a healthy score. The length of credit history matters as well, since older accounts in good standing demonstrate a longer track record of responsible borrowing than newly opened ones. Applying for multiple new loans or credit cards within a short period generates several hard inquiries, each of which can cause a small, temporary dip in the score. Finally, having a healthy mix of secured and unsecured credit, such as a combination of a home loan and a credit card, is viewed more favorably than relying on a single type of credit product.
